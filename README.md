<h1>Project README</h1>
<h2>Project Overview</h2>

<p>This project is for the development of a professional, accessible and inclusive website for Inclusive Access.</p>

<p>The initial website should provide a clear, modern and user-friendly online presence that communicates who we are, what we offer, and how clients can get in touch. The design should reflect the organisation's focus on accessibility, inclusion and professional service delivery.</p>

<p>The initial scope is intended as a starting point. The website structure, content and visual direction can be refined through discussion and review as the project progresses.</p>

<h2>Initial Website Structure</h2>

The proposed initial pages are:

<h3>Landing / Home Page</h3>

Clear introduction to Inclusive Access
Strong visual representation of the brand
Concise overview of services
Clear calls to action

About Us

Introduction to Inclusive Access
Background and purpose
Relevant experience, values and approach

Services

Overview of services provided
Clear, accessible descriptions of each service
Appropriate use of graphics or supporting visual elements

Contact

Contact information
Enquiry/contact form where appropriate
This information may also be incorporated into other pages rather than requiring a standalone page.

The final navigation and page structure can be discussed and agreed upon during the design process.

Design Direction

The website should have a professional, clean and accessible appearance, with a clear separation between written content and graphical elements.

Typography
Primary font: Arial
Minimum font size: 12pt
The "I" in "Inclusive", consistent with the logo, should use Roboto Slab.
The typography should remain easy to read across desktop, tablet and mobile devices.
Text should have sufficient spacing and contrast to support accessibility and readability.
Branding

The existing/AI-generated "Inclusive Access" logo provides a reference point for the visual identity.

The website should develop this identity into a cohesive design rather than simply reproducing the current AI-generated elements.

No watermarks should be used anywhere on the website.

Website Design References

The following websites are provided as examples of the preferred general design direction:
"https://www.disabilityaccessconsultants.com.au/"
"https://asnpl.com.au/"

These websites are intended as visual and structural references only. Their drop-down/navigation elements are excluded from the preferred design reference.

The final website should have its own identity while drawing inspiration from the clean, professional and accessible presentation of these examples.

Content and Visual Presentation

The website should prioritise clarity and ease of navigation.

Key requirements include:

Clear separation of text and graphics.
Consistent visual hierarchy.
Easy-to-read typography.
Appropriate use of whitespace.
Professional and uncluttered page layouts.
Responsive presentation across different screen sizes.
No unnecessary watermarks or distracting visual elements.
Accessibility should be considered throughout the design and content presentation.
Email Signature

A new email signature should also be developed to complement the website's visual identity.

The current email signature is AI-generated and should be replaced with a more polished design that:

Matches the website branding.
Uses the same or complementary typography and visual elements.
Presents contact details clearly.
Maintains a professional appearance.
Works reliably across common email clients.

The email signature should form part of the overall brand identity rather than appearing as a separate design.

Design Development

The requirements above are intended to establish the initial direction rather than represent a final design specification.

A draft layout should be developed for review, after which the following can be discussed and refined:

Page layouts
Navigation
Branding and colour palette
Typography
Imagery and graphics
Content structure
Calls to action
Contact functionality
Mobile/responsive presentation
Email signature design

Further information, clarification and content can be provided as required during the design process.

Next Steps

The immediate objective is to establish an initial website layout and visual direction based on the requirements and references above.

Once an initial draft is available, it can be reviewed and discussed before proceeding with the detailed design and development.

A call can be arranged if additional information or clarification is required.
